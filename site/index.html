Release notes

What's new

Every release

Method Budget 0.4.0

A schedule's days drive your globals, a shared budget can say who may change what, one person's cutoffs count once across accounts, and incentive programs show what they are worth before you apply them. Reviewers can comment on a copy, and the app works better with a screen reader.

  • Import Schedule Days. File > Import > Import Schedule Days… reads the days file Method Schedule exports. A review shows each figure with the global it sets, the value now and the new value. Total shoot days go to SHOOT_DAYS; days at a location, on a set, in a week or for a cast member go to globals you pick. Applying is one undo step.
  • Import again when the schedule changes. Each global remembers which figure it came from, so the next import ticks it again and says what changed. A figure that has left the schedule is never set to 0 unless you pick it.
  • Who may change what. In a budget shared through a folder, the owner can make each person an admin, an editor, an editor of some categories only, actuals only, comments only or read only, and can make an admin a co-owner. Changes beyond someone's role are refused on every computer, and the app lists them with who, when and why.
  • Good to know before you set the first role. Roles stop people changing what they shouldn't by accident. They don't stop someone who edits the shared folder's files directly. Everyone sharing the budget needs 0.4.0 or later once a role is set.
  • One cutoff per person. Give lines a person, and that person's fringe cutoffs count once across every account they are paid in, shared over their lines by pay. A new Person column shows how close each person is to each cutoff.
  • Incentive estimates by location. A credit can be limited to locations, and every credit, applied or not, now shows its qualified spend and an estimate after caps and the broker's discount. Budget Setup's Credits tab puts the programs side by side, next to the spend at each location. You write the programs from the public rules; nothing is bundled.

Features

Everything a production budget needs

All of this is built and in the app today. Open a group to see what is in it; the dashed box at the end is what comes next.

Budgeting engine 9 Topsheet, accounts and detail lines, Money to the cent, Formulas and globals, and more
  • Topsheet, accounts and detail lines The familiar three levels, with every total rolling up live as you type.
  • Money to the cent Every amount is kept in whole cents and rounds the way production budgets have always rounded, with an exact mode if you want one.
  • Formulas and globals Amount, quantity and rate cells take formulas and named globals; change a global and every line that uses it follows.
  • Schedule-linked quantities Read a schedule's day counts (shoot days, days per location, set, week or cast member) and set your globals from them, after a review of every change.
  • Currencies Any line can be in its own currency, converted to the budget's base currency at rates you set.
  • Groups and what-ifs Tag lines into groups, leave a group out of the total to see a what-if, and get back to the exact cent when you put it back.
  • Explain this number Trace any total down to the lines, fringes, cutoffs, ranges and rounding steps that make it up.
  • Overtime helper Work out a day or a week of straight time, overtime bands and sixth and seventh days, then write the pay into the line.
  • Fast on big budgets An edit recalculates in about 5 ms on a 5,000-line budget.
Fringes, globals, charges and credits 8 Fringes with cutoffs and ranges, One cutoff per person, Globals with details, and more
  • Fringes with cutoffs and ranges Percent and flat fringes, cutoffs per earner, fringe ranges across several lines, unit-hour flats and four ways to post them.
  • One cutoff per person Name the person on each of their lines, in any account, and a fringe cutoff such as a wage base applies once to everything they are paid.
  • Globals with details Give a global a description, a unit and decimals, show it by name in cells, and see the formulas behind a selection.
  • Contractual charges Percent and flat charges with exclusions, a live preview while you type, and the percent shown on the topsheet if you want it.
  • Credits Credit rows show gross, excluded fringes, net and how far you are from a cap.
  • Incentive estimates by location Limit a credit to the locations where the money is spent and see every program's estimate side by side, applied or not, from rates and caps you enter yourself.
  • Apply panel Select lines, then tick fringes, groups, sets and locations on or off, with live totals for the selection.
  • In-use counts Every setup list shows how many lines use each item and what they cost, warns before you delete one in use, and finds them in a click.
Editing, split view and history 9 Keyboard-first grid, Type-ahead and add on the fly, Never lose work, and more
  • Keyboard-first grid Drill in, climb out, step between accounts, fill down and move rows without touching the mouse; a shortcut sheet lists every key.
  • Type-ahead and add on the fly Units, currencies, sets, locations and globals complete as you type, and a new one can be added right from the cell.
  • Never lose work Autosave into the file, recovery copies for untitled budgets, rolling backups and a restore after a crash.
  • Budget tabs and split view Open several budgets as tabs, each with its own undo, and put two side by side in one window.
  • History list Every undo step is listed by name and time; click one to go back to it, or save it as a version.
  • Find and replace Search descriptions, account numbers, fringes, globals, groups, notes and formulas, then replace only in the results you tick, as one undo step.
  • Copy rows with everything on them Rows keep their fringes, groups, notes, style and ranges when copied, even between budgets, with a review of what the paste will change.
  • Notes Dated notes on lines, accounts and categories, and a notes list that works as a review checklist.
  • Signed and notarized Mac app The Mac app is signed and notarized by Apple, so it opens like any other app with no extra security step.
Versions, compare, diff and merge 8 Named versions, Compare up to 16 budgets, Original and variance columns, and more
  • Named versions Save, rename, annotate and restore versions, all kept inside the budget file.
  • Compare up to 16 budgets Versions or other files side by side at topsheet and account level, with sum, average and variance columns, down to detail lines.
  • Original and variance columns Keep a locked baseline and see each account's change from it right in the grid.
  • What changed A plain-words diff of two budgets or versions: every changed line, field and setup item, with the money it moved.
  • Three-way merge Two copies that changed apart merge on their own where they touched different things; real clashes are listed for you to pick.
  • Share-ready copies Save a dated snapshot, optionally without notes or private info, that opens read-only and compares back against the original.
  • Review files A reviewer comments on lines, accounts and categories of a budget they can't change; the comments come back in a small file beside it, and you merge each one in as a dated note or set it aside.
  • Small version history Versions can be stored as compact patches, so ten versions of a large budget take about the space of one.
Actuals and cost reports 2 Import actuals, Cost report
  • Import actuals Bring in transactions from CSV, tab-delimited or Excel accounting exports with a column mapping, duplicate checks and a review of unknown accounts.
  • Cost report Budget, actual to date, estimate to complete and variance by account, with each account's estimate method your choice.
Reports, PDF and spreadsheets 6 Reports saved in the budget, Live preview, More than the topsheet, and more
  • Reports saved in the budget Pick sections, accounts and layout, save them as named presets with header and footer fields, and print straight to PDF.
  • Live preview The setup dialog shows the printed pages as you change them, with page navigation and a jump list of sections.
  • More than the topsheet Category summaries, account detail, setup tables, chart of accounts, group totals, budget information and a notes list.
  • Spreadsheet export One Excel workbook with topsheet, accounts, detail and setup tables, plus CSV and tab-delimited files for accounting.
  • Spreadsheet import Turn any department's Excel, CSV or tab-delimited sheet into budget lines with a column mapping and a line-by-line check of the totals.
  • Import from other budgeting apps Open a budget from another program's JSON, XML, Excel or tab-delimited export, with a line-by-line check against that program's own totals.
Setup library and templates 6 Start from another budget, Personal setup library, Tag presets and line blocks, and more
  • Start from another budget Import setup items from any budget, with what they depend on, or start a new budget from a template.
  • Personal setup library Keep your fringes, globals, groups and the rest in a library file of your own and copy them in and out with a conflict review.
  • Tag presets and line blocks Save a set of tags or a block of lines once and apply or insert them anywhere.
  • Rename everywhere Rename a fringe, global, group or unit and every reference follows, formulas included, as one undo step.
  • Renumber accounts Change the account numbering pattern and preview every old and new number before it applies.
  • Sub-budgets Make a filtered copy by categories, accounts, groups, sets or locations, for a department or a unit.
Command line and git 4 Open file format, Scriptable command line, Batch edits, and more
  • Open file format Budgets are .mthd files: plain, documented JSON that diffs cleanly and that any tool can read.

Yours to keep

Your budget is a file you own

A budget is the record of months of decisions. It should outlive any app, any subscription and any company, including this one.

  • Plain, documented JSON. Budgets are .mthd files with a published specification. Any tool can read them, today or in twenty years.
  • Exact money. Amounts are whole cents and rates are written as typed, so the same budget always produces the same bytes.
  • Made for version control. Files diff line by line, and a git merge driver combines two people's edits to the same budget.
  • On your disk, not ours. Save a budget wherever you keep your work. Nothing is uploaded, and the app works with no connection.
  • Free, for good. Open source under MIT or Apache-2.0, with no subscription and no account.
  • Nothing invents your numbers. There is no generative AI in the app: every figure comes from what you typed and the rules you set.

Read the file format

// one detail line, as written in the file
{
  "id": 1042,
  "description": "Director of photography",
  "amount": "PREP_WKS + SHOOT_WKS",
  "unit": "Weeks",
  "rate": "4850",
  "fringes": ["HP", "PT"],
  "groups": ["Main unit"]
}

Formulas are stored as typed, and fields left at their default are left out, so a change to one line is a change to a few lines of the file.

Keyboard first

Hands stay on the keys

Budgeting is typing. Drill into an account, climb back to the topsheet, step to the next account, fill down and move rows without reaching for the mouse.

Type-ahead completes units, currencies, sets, locations and globals as you type, and a new one can be added right from the cell. A shortcut sheet lists every key.

Command line and coding agents

The same engine, without the window

The method command reads and writes budgets with no window and no network, so budgets fit into scripts, build steps and automated checks.

$ method-budget show my-film.mthd                  # the topsheet
$ method-budget check my-film.mthd                 # recalculate, report problems
$ method-budget totals my-film.mthd --json         # every total, in cents
$ method-budget explain my-film.mthd net           # how a total is made up
$ method-budget diff old.mthd new.mthd             # what changed, in plain words
$ method-budget report my-film.mthd -o budget.pdf  # a PDF report

FAQ

Questions

Is it an alternative to Movie Magic Budgeting?

Yes: a free, open-source one that runs on your own computer and opens Movie Magic Budgeting's exports (JSON, XML, Excel and tab-delimited), checking every figure as it comes in. See Bring your budgets with you.

Is Method Budget affiliated with other budgeting software?

No. Method Budget is an independent, clean-room project, not affiliated with Entertainment Partners or Movie Magic Budgeting.

Can it open budgets from other apps?

Yes, from the open files they export, never their native files. A Movie Magic Budgeting budget comes in from its JSON, XML, Excel or tab-delimited export (JSON carries the most), and any spreadsheet comes in with a column mapping, each with a line-by-line check against the totals in the file. See Bring your budgets with you.

Where does my data live?

On your own computer, in files you save wherever you like. There is no account and no cloud. The app goes online only to check for its own updates, and you can turn that off.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Method Budget is free and open source, dual-licensed under MIT or Apache-2.0, so it can't be taken away from you.

Which computers does it run on?

The Mac comes first: the app is built and used there every day. Windows and Linux installers are built from the same code and are waiting on a hands-on test.

Can I script it or keep budgets in git?

Yes. The method command line checks budgets, prints totals, draws reports and makes batch edits, and its git merge driver combines two people's edits to the same budget.

How can I help?

Try it, report what breaks, and send patches. Please never attach a real budget to an issue: an invented one that shows the problem is always enough. Start with the repository.
